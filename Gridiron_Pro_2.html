Weekly training plan

Sessions are designed for about 45–70 minutes including warm-up and cooldown. Start with the bodyweight/outdoor version when equipment is unavailable. Do not try to make every day an all-out day.

Speed work should be high quality with full recovery between runs. If sprint form slows down, end the fast reps. Avoid hard sprinting on slippery, uneven or crowded ground.

Field guide

Warm-up before every session

  • 3–5 minutes easy walk or jog.
  • Leg swings, arm circles and ankle mobility.
  • Marching, skips and controlled bodyweight squats.
  • Two or three gradual build-up runs before faster running.

DT technique cues

  • Stance: balanced, comfortable base; eyes up.
  • Get-off: react to the ball movement; stay under control.
  • Hands: strike with hands inside and elbows active.
  • Leverage: keep a strong base; don't stand straight up.
  • Finish: disengage under control and pursue the play.

Practice contact technique only with a qualified coach, proper equipment and safe supervision.

Recovery basics

  • Keep both recovery days genuinely easier.
  • Eat regular meals and snacks; include protein, carbohydrates, fruit/vegetables and fluids.
  • Drink water throughout the day; use extra fluids during hot, sweaty sessions.
  • For teens, aim for 8–10 hours of sleep.
  • Tell a parent/guardian and coach if you feel unusually exhausted or unwell.

Weekly structure

Day 1: Acceleration + lower-body strength

Day 2: Conditioning + DT movement/technique

Day 3: Recovery / mobility

Day 4: Explosiveness + upper-body strength

Day 5: Agility + repeat-effort conditioning

Day 6: DT technique + full-body athletic work

Day 7: Recovery
